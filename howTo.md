2. How each app works









1. Forest — focus through motivation
Best for building focus habits
How it works: You choose a focus duration and plant a virtual tree. The tree grows while you focus. Depending on your settings, leaving the focus session can cause it to wither.
- Pomodoro-style focus timer.
- Virtual forest, rewards and achievements.
- Focus history and statistics.
- App blocking and scheduled restrictions on supported configurations.
- Group focus with friends and optional real-tree planting.
Example: Start a 45-minute work session, keep distracting apps out of reach, and finish with a tree added to your forest.

Forest primarily encourages you to stay focused; it is not a dedicated Shorts/Reels-only blocker.

2. NoScroll — block the content, not the whole app
Best match for your Shorts/Reels goal
How it works: It uses Android's Accessibility Service to detect supported short-video screens and trigger your selected blocking action. This generally requires enabling its accessibility permission.
- Blocks supported YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, TikTok-style feeds and other listed platforms.
- Block All, Curious Mode and One Reel Mode, according to its published feature descriptions.
- Can close the player, exit the app or lock the screen, depending on configuration.
- Also provides app/site blocking and a Reels-block counter.
- Some features require premium access.
Example: Open Instagram to reply to a message, but when you enter Reels, the blocker intervenes while ordinary Instagram features remain usable.

Exact coverage depends on supported app versions and how the feed is displayed.

3. one sec — interrupt the automatic habit
Best for reducing impulsive opening
How it works: When you try to open a configured distracting app, it inserts a pause before letting you continue. This may involve breathing, a small task, or a prompt to consider your intention.
- Breathing and other configurable interventions.
- Delayed or repeated interventions.
- Re-interventions after a chosen amount of time in a target app.
- Supported removal/blocking of in-app distractions, including Reels and Shorts.
- App/site blocking features where supported.
Example: You tap Instagram automatically. one sec makes you pause, then asks whether you actually want to use it. If you keep scrolling, another intervention can appear after the configured interval.

In-app distraction removal and other advanced features depend on Android support and configuration.

4. Opal — enforce focus rules
Best for scheduled restrictions
How it works: You choose which apps or websites to block, when the restriction should apply, and how difficult it should be to interrupt the session.
- Immediate focus sessions and scheduled sessions.
- Daily cumulative usage limits.
- A configurable period of blocking after the limit is reached.
- Different break and cancellation protection levels.
- Screen-time and focus reports.
Example: Set Instagram and YouTube to be blocked during work hours, or allow a limited amount of daily usage before blocking them.

Opal's Android documentation confirms sessions, schedules and time limits. Its normal app blocking should not be confused with reliable Shorts-only detection.
